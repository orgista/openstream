## What the REA established
- Netflix's phone billboard uses art made for portrait (VerticalBillboard / VerticalStoryArt), centre-cropped in a
  region capped at min(65 % of screen height, 1.45 × width); the logo is a separate image, centred; the gradient is
  the art's dominant colour from the title stack down. It never shows a landscape backdrop cropped to portrait.
- Ours (`AppleHeroPortraitArt.swift:73`): textless TMDB poster → textless TMDB backdrop → intact provider card →
  black, decided within 1.5 s and remembered for the process. A 16:9 backdrop in our 73 %-tall hero keeps only
  35.55 % of its width (`AppleHomePhoneHeroLayout.swift:48`), so the subject is cut or off-centre whenever the
  poster isn't there in time and the backdrop fallback wins. The focal point (`AppleHeroFocalPoint.swift:20`) is a
  point, not a subject box, so "focal inside the crop" never guaranteed the subject fits.

## Acceptance rule (from the REA; the fix is judged by this, on the owner's iOS 18.6 setup)
1. A 2:3 portrait poster, when one exists for the title, is always the hero art and is shown whole (aspect-fit,
   centred, no stretch, letterboxing filled by the scrim colour) or aspect-filled only if ≥ 95 % of its width stays
   visible.
2. A landscape backdrop is never cropped to the portrait hero if that would keep < 60 % of its width; in that case
   use the intact poster card fallback (`AppleHeroPortraitArt.swift:75`) or aspect-fit the backdrop with the scrim
   colour behind it.
3. The remembered decision is replaced when a poster arrives later (do not pin a backdrop crop for the whole process
   when the poster answer was merely late).
4. The title treatment, the 32 %→86 % fade and the 73 % hero height stay as they are.
5. The trailer cover uses the same rect as the art it covers (`AppleHomePhonePortraitStage.swift:195,248`).
